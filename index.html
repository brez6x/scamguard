This preview's scanning engine

This deployed preview runs a real, transparent URL-structure heuristic engine entirely in your browser — it looks at the actual link you paste (protocol, hostname, TLD, subdomains, known shortener domains, brand-lookalike patterns, and similar structural signals). It does not fabricate domain-age, certificate, or reputation data.

A server-side check also runs on every scan: domain age/registrar via a live RDAP (WHOIS) lookup, safe redirect-chain following (SSRF-guarded — private/internal targets are refused before anything is fetched), and, when configured, a Google Safe Browsing lookup against known malware and phishing URLs. TLS certificate chain inspection is not yet connected. If a live check fails, times out, or isn't configured, that specific field is honestly marked Not available with the real reason — never faked.

About

Why ScamGuard exists

Fake online stores, phishing pages, and impersonation sites are built to look convincing in the seconds before someone enters a password or a card number. ScamGuard exists to slow that moment down — giving people a clear, evidence-based second opinion before they click, sign in, or pay.

ScamGuard doesn't claim to catch every scam or guarantee that any website is safe. It's built to be honest about what it actually knows: every signal shown in a report is one ScamGuard's checks genuinely found, and every check it couldn't run is labeled as unavailable rather than guessed at.

The goal is simple: help ordinary people make a more informed decision, in plain language, before something goes wrong.

Privacy Policy

Last updated August 21, 2026. Plain-language summary of what ScamGuard does and doesn't do with your data.

What this preview collects: Nothing is sent to a server. When you scan a URL, ScamGuard analyzes the link entirely in your browser. The only thing saved is your scan history, and it's saved on your own device, not on ScamGuard's servers — see below.

Scan history: Each scan you run (domain, score, date) is stored locally using your browser's on-device storage so you can review it later in Scan History. This data is tied to your device/browser, not to an account, and is never transmitted anywhere. You can delete any entry, or all of them, at any time from the Scan History page.

URLs you scan: ScamGuard does not log, store, or transmit the URLs you analyze to any external service. Because links can contain sensitive information (session tokens, email addresses, tracking identifiers), avoid pasting a URL that isn't meant to be shared if you're ever using a version of ScamGuard that syncs history to a server.

Accounts and payments: Login and paid plans are not connected in this preview — no account data or payment information is collected because those systems don't exist yet.

Future versions: If ScamGuard adds server-side scanning (domain age, certificates, reputation databases) or accounts, this policy will be updated to explain exactly what's collected, why, how long it's kept, and how to delete it — before those features go live, not after.

Contact: Questions about this policy can be sent to the site owner listed in the footer.
